What we check in a technical SEO audit

  • Indexed pages. A site: search and Search Console data to see how many pages Google has indexed. Most local businesses need at least 30 useful, indexed pages to compete.
  • Crawl errors and redirects. Broken links, redirect chains, 404 pages and blocked resources.
  • Title tags and headings. Whether each page targets a clear service and location, with one H1 and logical H2s.
  • NAP consistency. Your business name, address and phone shown on the site and matching your Google Business Profile.
  • Schema markup. LocalBusiness, Service, FAQ and Breadcrumb structured data, checked in the Schema.org validator.
  • Speed and Core Web Vitals. Mobile load times and page experience issues.
  • Mobile usability. Layout, tap targets and font sizes on phones.
  • Duplicate and thin content. Pages that compete with each other or don't give Google enough to rank.

Common easy wins

Small-business websites often share the same quick fixes. The homepage title doesn't mention the main service or city. The H1 is a slogan instead of a description. The footer has no address or phone number. There's no LocalBusiness schema, no embedded map and only a handful of indexed pages.

Each of these takes minutes to fix and gives Google clearer signals about what you do and where. We list them first so you see progress quickly, then move on to the deeper issues.

What you receive

  • Audit report. Every issue, why it matters and how to fix it, in plain language.
  • Priority list. Fixes ranked by impact and effort, easy wins first.
  • Walkthrough call. We go through the findings and answer questions.
  • Optional implementation. We make the fixes for you, then re-check.

Is Technical SEO Audit right for your business?

A technical audit is worth doing before any major SEO investment, and whenever results drop without an obvious reason. It's particularly useful in these cases.

  • Before a redesign or migration. Know what ranks today so nothing valuable is lost in the move.
  • After a traffic drop. Find whether indexing, technical errors or competitors are behind it.
  • When SEO isn't working. Content and links won't help if Google can't crawl or understand your site.

What affects the cost of Technical SEO Audit

Audits are quoted as a fixed project, with implementation quoted separately if you'd like us to make the fixes.

  • Site size. A 20-page brochure site is quicker to audit than a store with thousands of URLs.
  • Platform. Some platforms limit what can be changed, which shapes the recommendations.
  • Data access. Search Console and analytics access makes the audit faster and more precise.
  • Implementation. Whether you want a report only, or the fixes done for you.

Technical SEO Audit FAQs

Ask anything else in the form below and we'll cover it on our call.

What's the difference between a technical SEO audit and the free SEO audit?

The free audit is a quick review of your biggest opportunities. A full technical audit crawls every page and covers indexing, schema, speed and content in depth.

How many pages should my website have indexed?

For most local businesses, at least 30 useful pages, made up of service pages, area pages and helpful content. Competitive markets often need more.

Do I need access to Google Search Console?

It helps a lot. If you don't have it set up, we'll set it up and verify it for you.

Can my developer make the fixes?

Yes. The report explains each fix clearly enough for any developer to follow.

How often should I audit my website?

Once a year, and after any redesign, platform change or big drop in traffic.

Will the audit tell me why a competitor outranks me?

It covers your site in depth and includes a comparison with the top competitors for your main keyword, so you can see where the gaps are.

Do you check schema markup?

Yes. We test your LocalBusiness, Service, FAQ and Breadcrumb markup and fix anything missing or invalid.
