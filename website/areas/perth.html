Local search across a long metro area

A plumber based in Osborne Park won't naturally rank in Baldivis, and a clinic in Fremantle won't appear for searches in Midland. Perth businesses that serve a wide area need a deliberate plan: a well-defined service area, pages for each region and reviews from customers across it.

Perth's economy also includes a large resources and mining services sector. Many businesses here sell to other businesses, where search terms are more specific and buyers research longer. For these, detailed service pages and technical content often matter more than the map pack.

How we approach SEO for Perth businesses

We run a heatmap that covers your full service area rather than just your home suburb, so you can see exactly where visibility drops off. Then we compare the top-ranking businesses on reviews, photos, categories and services, and fix your profile's gaps.

On the website, we add your service and Perth to title tags and headings, put your details in the footer, add schema and an embedded map, and build area pages for each region you serve. Citations on Australian directories and any WA-specific industry associations complete the picture.

Perth service businesses usually start with local SEO by region. B2B suppliers benefit from detailed service content, and Meta ads are a quick way to reach new suburbs.

Local SEO checklist for Perth businesses

Whether you work with us or not, these are the steps that make the biggest difference to local rankings in Perth.

  1. Define your real service area. Perth is long and spread out. List the regions you actually travel to, rather than the whole metro area.
  2. Build a page for each corridor. Northern suburbs, southern suburbs, the hills and Fremantle often need separate pages.
  3. Set WA business hours. Make sure your hours and response times are listed in Perth time.
  4. Collect reviews across the city. Reviews from customers in different suburbs help Google connect you to them.
  5. Write for B2B buyers. If you supply industry, create detailed service pages that answer technical buying questions.
  6. Keep your NAP consistent. Check that old addresses or phone numbers aren't still listed on directories.
